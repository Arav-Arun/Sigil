How it works

From a face to a proof

Five stages. Each one is inspectable, and the two that decide anything, the face comparison and the hash, are the two you can re-run yourself.

  1. 1

    Detect and encode

    SCRFD finds the face, five landmarks align it, and ArcFace turns it into 512 numbers. Those numbers are biometric data: they stay in memory, and are never logged, never written to the evidence, never put on a chain.

  2. 2

    Search the live web

    One Google Lens call returns visual matches, the pages that discuss the image, and the name it inferred. If that comes back thin, a second wave pivots on the name across social platforms and re-queries a head-and-shoulders crop.

  3. 3

    Fetch and compare

    Every candidate's image is downloaded and every face in it is compared against yours. The decision is a calibrated distance, so a result that ranked first in the search cannot be promoted past the face check.

  4. 4

    Seal the evidence

    Every field is serialised to one canonical byte sequence (RFC 8785), hashed into a domain-separated leaf, and combined into a Merkle tree. One 32-byte root now stands for the whole finding.

  5. 5

    Anchor on Sepolia

    Only the root is submitted. No image, no name, no post text, no embedding. What the chain adds is the one thing you cannot fake locally: an independent witness that this exact root existed at this exact time.

Nothing personal is stored

The contract holds a hash, an address, a timestamp and a format version. Biometric data on an immutable public ledger is a permanent harm, so none is written.

Checking needs no wallet

Re-verification is a read-only blockchain lookup. A proof that required the claimant's private key would not be a proof.

A change is named, not just noticed

A single hash would only tell you something changed. The inclusion proof identifies the exact detail, for example, the post address.
